## Introduction

In this lesson we introduce the core idea: focus on improving judgment rather than predicting precise outcomes. In a world full of noise and uncertainty, the ability to sensibly assess situations and make decisions is more valuable than the skill of predicting the future with high precision.

### Why judgment is more important than prediction?

- Predictions often give a false sense of control, while reality is full of surprises.
- Judgment allows flexible responses to changing circumstances, while rigid forecasts can lead to wrong decisions.
- Even simple statistical models often outperform experts in prediction, but human judgment is essential for interpreting results and considering context.

### Example: Weather forecast and decision to go on a trip

Imagine the weather forecast predicts a 70% chance of rain tomorrow. A person focused only on prediction might decide: "Since there's more than 50% chance of rain, I'll stay home." However, good judgment considers additional factors:
- What are the consequences of getting wet during the trip? (e.g., mild discomfort vs. risk of catching a cold)
- What are the benefits of going out despite the rain? (e.g., meeting friends, physical activity)
- Are there ways to reduce the risk? (e.g., bringing an umbrella, choosing a route with shelter)

In this way, judgment integrates probability with values, goals, and context.

### How to improve judgment?

1. **Track your forecasts and outcomes** – keep a journal where you record your predictions and actual results. This helps you notice patterns of error.
2. **Seek disconfirming evidence** – actively search for information that contradicts your initial impression. This counters confirmation bias.
3. **Consider alternative explanations** – before accepting one hypothesis, think about several other possible interpretations of the data.
4. **Practice belief updating** – when new information appears, consciously adjust the weight assigned to different scenarios.
5. **Recognize emotional influences** – notice when fear, excitement, or fatigue affect your decisions, and introduce procedures that limit their impact.

### Reflection

Think about a recent important decision you made. Did you focus more on trying to predict the outcome, or on evaluating what actions would be best under different possible scenarios? How could you improve your approach next time?
