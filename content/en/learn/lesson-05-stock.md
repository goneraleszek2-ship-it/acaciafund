## Introduction

Financial markets are often seen as a place where you can quickly make money by predicting price directions. However, history shows that even professional analysts struggle to consistently outperform the market. Why? Because short-term price movements are largely unpredictable, while long-term value is created by business fundamentals.

### Why price forecasting is difficult?

- Stock prices reflect the expectations of all market participants about the future.
- New information arrives randomly and is instantly incorporated into prices (efficient market hypothesis).
- Overconfidence leads to focusing on a few "sure" forecasts, ignoring uncertainty.

### Judgment in stock analysis

Instead of trying to predict short-term price moves, better judgment focuses on:
1. **Assessing intrinsic value** – how much a company is worth based on its future cash flows.
2. **Qualitative analysis** – evaluating management, business model, competitive advantages (moat).
3. **Risk management** – determining how much you could lose in a pessimistic scenario.
4. **Distinguishing signal from noise** – identifying which information matters for long-term value.

### Example: Evaluating a tech company

Consider two companies in the same sector:
- Company A: high revenue growth but low profitability, aggressive accounting, management focused on short-term stock price.
- Company B: moderate growth, high margin, transparent communication, management with significant ownership stake.

Although Company A may show better quarterly results in the short run, judgment suggests Company B has better odds of creating lasting value due to superior business quality.

### How to develop investment judgment?

- **Keep an investment journal** – record investment theses, key assumptions, and outcomes. Review them regularly.
- **Focus on process, not outcome** – a good decision can lead to loss due to bad luck, and a bad decision sometimes yields profit. Evaluate the quality of the decision-making process.
- **Learn from mistakes** – analyze not only losing investments, but also those that succeeded for the wrong reasons.
- **Consider disconfirming evidence** – before making a decision, explicitly list three reasons why you might be wrong.
- **Limit transaction frequency** – fewer transactions mean fewer chances to make emotion-driven errors.

### Reflection

Think about a recent investment decision (or a similar decision, like choosing an educational course). Did you focus more on predicting short-term outcomes, or on assessing long-term value and risk? What assumptions did you make, and how could you test them?
